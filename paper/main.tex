%% Abstract (English) ----------------------------------------
\begin{abstract}
\textbf{Background.}
Skeletal maturity assessment is a cornerstone of pediatric endocrinology,
auxology, and forensic medicine. The Tanner-Whitehouse 2 (TW2) method scores
20 individual hand bones across maturation stages A through I and is the
gold standard in Latin America and Europe. Its manual execution is
time-consuming, highly operator-dependent, and no open-source software
currently replicates the complete TW2 pipeline with individual bone-level
interpretation.
\textbf{Objective.}
We present \textbf{BoneAgeTW2}, an end-to-end deep-learning system that
(1) automatically segments and identifies the 20 TW2 bones from a hand
radiograph, (2) classifies each bone into its maturation stage using an
EfficientNet-B3 classifier with 20 specialized heads, (3) computes TW2
RUS and Carpal scores, (4) estimates bone age with 90\% confidence
intervals, and (5) renders interactive Gaussian distribution curves.
\textbf{Dataset.}
We use the RSNA Pediatric Bone Age Challenge dataset \cite{rsna2017},
comprising 12,611 hand radiographs with bone age labels.
\textbf{Code:}
\url{https://github.com/jmmana/BoneAgeTW2}
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Bone age (skeletal age) is the biological age of the skeleton determined
from the degree of ossification of the hand and wrist bones in a standard
anteroposterior radiograph. Clinicians use it to diagnose growth disorders,
evaluate the effects of hormone therapy, estimate chronological age in
forensic contexts, and guide orthodontic and orthopedic
planning~\cite{grote2012}.

\subsection{Clinical Significance}

In Latin America and Spain, the Tanner-Whitehouse 2 (TW2) method is the
dominant standard for bone age assessment~\cite{tannerTW2}. Its defining
advantage over the atlas-based Greulich-Pyle (GP) method~\cite{gp1959} is
individual bone interpretation: each of 20 specific bones is assigned a
maturation stage (A--I), scored numerically, and summed into a composite
RUS (radius-ulna-short bones) or Carpal score that maps to bone age via
sex-specific reference curves.

\subsection{Problem Statement}

Despite the clinical value of TW2, its adoption is limited by three
practical barriers:
\begin{enumerate}[noitemsep]
  \item \textbf{Operator expertise}: Correctly staging 20 bones requires
    years of training; inter-rater variability can exceed $\pm 12$
    months~\cite{grote2012}.
  \item \textbf{Time cost}: Manual TW2 assessment takes 10--20 minutes
    per case.
  \item \textbf{Software gap}: Commercial solutions
    (BoneXpert~\cite{bonexpert}) are proprietary black boxes; open-source
    solutions (Deeplasia~\cite{deeplasia}) predict age end-to-end without
    individual bone staging.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Contribution}

BoneAgeTW2 bridges this gap by providing:
\begin{itemize}[noitemsep]
  \item The first open-source implementation of the complete TW2 method
    with AI-assisted bone-level staging.
  \item A pseudo-labeling strategy that extracts per-bone stage labels from
    globally-labeled bone age datasets.
  \item An interactive web application with Gaussian distribution curves
    reproducing the original TW2 graphical output.
  \item A fully documented REST API suitable for integration into hospital
    PACS systems.
\end{itemize}

\section{Background and Related Work}
\label{sec:background}

\subsection{Bone Age Assessment Methods}

\paragraph{Greulich-Pyle (GP).}
Compares the hand radiograph against an atlas of reference
images~\cite{gp1959}. Fast but subjective; validated primarily in
North American White populations.

\paragraph{Tanner-Whitehouse (TW1/TW2/TW3).}
A scoring system where each of $N$ bones is assigned a maturation stage
with a numeric score~\cite{tannerTW2,tw32001}. TW2 uses 20 bones; TW3
refines the reference curves using more recent European data. The
\emph{RUS} subscore (13 bones) is the primary predictor; the
\emph{Carpal} subscore (7 bones) provides complementary information.

\paragraph{Fels method.}
Probabilistic approach based on 98 measurements and observations of hand
bones~\cite{fels1988}. High precision but rarely used outside the USA.

\subsection{Deep Learning for Bone Age Assessment}

The RSNA 2017 Pediatric Bone Age Challenge~\cite{rsna2017} catalyzed a wave
of deep learning work. Winning solutions achieved MAE $\approx 4.3$--6
months using CNNs trained end-to-end. Subsequently:

\begin{itemize}[noitemsep]
  \item \textbf{Deeplasia}~\cite{deeplasia}: EfficientNet-based open-source
    model robust to skeletal dysplasias.
  \item \textbf{Rassmann et al.}~\cite{rassmann2023}: End-to-end deep
    learning; MAE $\approx 5.8$ months.
  \item \textbf{BoneXpert}~\cite{bonexpert}: Commercial CE-marked software
    using Active Shape Models.
  \item \textbf{CarpIAn}~\cite{carpian}: Argentinian hospital AI; no
    open-source release.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Age distribution.}
Bone ages range from 1 to 228 months (0--19 years), with a roughly
uniform distribution, enabling coverage of all TW2 maturation stages.
Each image is a left-hand anteroposterior radiograph; right-hand images
are horizontally flipped during preprocessing.

\paragraph{Image characteristics.}
Images were acquired across multiple clinical sites, resulting in
variability in exposure, contrast, resolution, and patient positioning.
This heterogeneity serves as implicit data augmentation for model
robustness.

\subsection{Limitations of the Dataset for TW2}

The RSNA dataset provides \emph{global} bone age labels (in months) but
no \emph{per-bone stage} annotations. This is the fundamental challenge:
the dataset contains the ground truth for the \emph{output} of TW2
(bone age) but not for the \emph{intermediate representation} (individual
bone stages). Our pseudo-labeling strategy addresses this limitation.

\section{Methodology}
\label{sec:methodology}

\subsection{Stage 1: Image Preprocessing}
\label{sec:preproc}

All radiographs undergo the following pipeline:

\begin{enumerate}[noitemsep]
  \item \textbf{Format loading}: DICOM files read with \texttt{pydicom};
    PNG/JPEG with OpenCV. MONOCHROME1 photometric interpretation is
    inverted so bones appear bright on dark background.
  \item \textbf{Pixel spacing extraction}: DICOM
    \texttt{PixelSpacing} metadata yields the true mm-per-pixel scale
    factor (\emph{digital escalimetry}). For non-DICOM inputs, users
    specify the scale factor manually; a default of 0.143 mm/px is
    assumed.
  \item \textbf{Hand orientation}: A brightness-mass heuristic detects
    whether the thumb is on the left or right, flipping right-hand images
    to a standardized left-hand orientation.
  \item \textbf{Contrast enhancement}: CLAHE (clip limit 2.0, tile
    size $8{\times}8$) improves bone margin visibility.
  \item \textbf{Resize and normalize}: Images resized to
    $512{\times}512$ pixels, normalized to $[0, 1]$.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Stage 2: Bone Region Detection}
\label{sec:detection}

We employ YOLOv8-small~\cite{yolov8} fine-tuned to detect 20 bounding
box regions corresponding to the TW2 bone set.

\paragraph{TW2 bone set.}
The 20 TW2 bones comprise two groups:
\begin{itemize}[noitemsep]
  \item \textbf{RUS (13 bones)}: radius, ulna; metacarpals I, III, V;
    proximal phalanges I, III, V; middle phalanges III, V; distal
    phalanges I, III, V.
  \item \textbf{Carpal (7 bones)}: capitate, hamate, triquetral, lunate,
    scaphoid, trapezoid, trapezium.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Anatomical priors.}
The 20 TW2 bones occupy known relative positions within the hand. We
encode these as normalized bounding box priors
$(c_x, c_y, w, h) \in [0,1]^4$. These serve as: (a) initial anchor
positions for YOLO fine-tuning, and (b) a robust fallback detection
mechanism when model weights are unavailable.

\subsection{Stage 3: Maturation Stage Classification}
\label{sec:classifier}

\subsubsection{Architecture}

We employ an EfficientNet-B3~\cite{efficientnet} backbone pretrained on
ImageNet, followed by 20 independent classification heads. Each head:
\begin{equation}
  f_b(\mathbf{x}) = W_b \, \text{ReLU}(\text{Drop}_{0.3}(W_1 \phi(\mathbf{x})))
\end{equation}
where $\phi(\cdot)$ is the backbone feature extractor,
$W_1 \in \mathbb{R}^{d \times 256}$ is a shared projection, and
$W_b \in \mathbb{R}^{256 \times K_b}$ projects to $K_b \in \{8, 9\}$
stage logits.

\subsubsection{Pseudo-label Generation}

The absence of per-bone stage labels in RSNA is addressed via a
probabilistic assignment strategy:

\begin{center}
\fbox{\begin{minipage}{0.92\columnwidth}
\textbf{Algorithm 1:} TW2 Pseudo-label Generation\\[2pt]
\textbf{Input:} age $t$ (months), sex $s$, bone $b$,
  Gaussian params $\{\mu_{b,s,k}, \sigma_{b,s,k}\}$\\[2pt]
\textbf{for} each stage $k \in \mathcal{S}_b$ \textbf{do}\\
\quad $d_k \leftarrow \mathcal{N}(t \mid \mu_{b,s,k}, \sigma_{b,s,k})$\\
\textbf{end for}\\
$p_k \leftarrow d_k / \sum_{k'} d_{k'}$\\
\textbf{return} $\hat{k} = \arg\max_k\; p_k$
\end{minipage}}
\end{center}

The Gaussian parameters encode the mean age and SD at which 50\% of
children of sex $s$ reach stage $k$ for bone $b$, derived from published
TW2/TW3 reference data~\cite{tannerTW2,tw32001}.

\subsection{Stage 4: TW2 Scoring}
\label{sec:scoring}

Given the predicted stage vector $\{k_b\}_{b=1}^{20}$:
\begin{align}
  S_{\text{RUS}} &= \sum_{b \in \mathcal{B}_\text{RUS}} w_{b,k_b}
  \label{eq:rus}\\
  S_{\text{Carpal}} &= \sum_{b \in \mathcal{B}_\text{Carpal}} w_{b,k_b}
  \label{eq:carpal}
\end{align}

where $w_{b,k}$ is the published TW2 numeric score. Raw scores are
normalized to $[0, 1000]$:
\begin{equation}
  \tilde{S} = \frac{S}{\displaystyle\sum_b \max_k w_{b,k}} \times 1000
\end{equation}

Bone age is read from sex-specific lookup tables via linear interpolation:
\begin{equation}
  \hat{t} = 0.75 \cdot \text{BA}_\text{RUS}(\tilde{S}_\text{RUS}, s)
          + 0.25 \cdot \text{BA}_\text{Carpal}(\tilde{S}_\text{Carpal}, s)
\end{equation}

The 90\% confidence interval is:
\begin{equation}
  \text{CI}_{90\%} \approx \hat{t} \pm 1.645 \cdot \sigma(\tilde{S}_\text{RUS})
\end{equation}

\subsection{Stage 5: Gaussian Distribution Visualization}

For each of the 20 bones, overlapping Gaussian curves display the
reference probability density of each stage across the age axis:
\begin{equation}
  p(k \mid t, b, s) \propto
  \mathcal{N}(t \mid \mu_{b,s,k}, \sigma_{b,s,k})
\end{equation}

The detected stage is highlighted with increased opacity. A vertical
reference line marks the patient's chronological age, allowing the
clinician to immediately visualize whether the bone is advanced, delayed,
or appropriate for the patient's age.

\section{System Architecture}
\label{sec:architecture}

\subsection{Backend -- FastAPI REST API}

The inference server is implemented in Python 3.11 with
FastAPI~\cite{fastapi}. Primary endpoint:

\begin{lstlisting}[language=bash,caption={API endpoint.}]
POST /analyze
  multipart/form-data:
    image     : file (DICOM/PNG/JPEG)
    sex       : "M" | "F"
    scale_factor : float (optional)
    chronological_age_months : float (opt.)

  Response JSON:
  { bone_age_months, bone_age_years,
    confidence_interval, rus_score,
    carpal_score, stages, bone_scores,
    annotated_image_b64, gaussian_data }
\end{lstlisting}

A second endpoint \texttt{POST /analyze/pdf} returns a PDF clinical
report. Reference endpoints expose the TW2 tables and Gaussian parameters
for external use.

\subsection{Frontend -- React Web Application}

Built with React 18, Vite, TypeScript, TailwindCSS, and Recharts.
Features three tabs:
\begin{enumerate}[noitemsep]
  \item \textbf{Annotated X-ray}: radiograph with 20 color-coded bounding
    boxes labeled with bone name and predicted stage.
  \item \textbf{TW2 Score Table}: per-bone stage, numeric score, and
    group (RUS/Carpal); summary with bone age and 90\% CI.
  \item \textbf{Gaussian Curves}: interactive Recharts area plot
    with bone selector, reproducing the classic TW2 graphical output.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Training Pipeline}

\begin{enumerate}[noitemsep]
  \item \texttt{01\_explore\_rsna.ipynb}: Data exploration.
  \item \texttt{02\_pseudo\_label\_generation.py}: Assign TW2 stages to
    all 12,611 RSNA images.
  \item \texttt{03\_train\_bone\_detector.py}: YOLOv8-s fine-tuning.
  \item \texttt{04\_train\_stage\_classifier.py}: EfficientNet-B3
    training with label smoothing.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Evaluation Protocol}
\label{sec:evaluation}

\subsection{Bone Age Estimation Accuracy}

\begin{align}
  \text{MAE} &= \tfrac{1}{N}\textstyle\sum_{i=1}^N |\hat{t}_i - t_i| \\
  \text{RMSE} &= \sqrt{\tfrac{1}{N}\textstyle\sum_{i=1}^N (\hat{t}_i - t_i)^2}
\end{align}

Reported separately for male/female and age groups $<$6y, 6--12y, $>$12y.

\subsection{Stage Classification Quality}

For a subset of 300 manually annotated cases:
\begin{itemize}[noitemsep]
  \item Per-bone accuracy (exact stage match).
  \item Quadratic weighted Cohen's $\kappa$.
  \item Confusion matrices for the 5 most clinically critical bones.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Comparison Baselines}

\begin{enumerate}[noitemsep]
  \item \textbf{Deeplasia}~\cite{deeplasia}: end-to-end CNN.
  \item \textbf{Manual TW2}: radiologist protocol.
  \item \textbf{GP atlas}: Greulich-Pyle.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{table}[H]

\scriptsize
\begin{tabularx}{\columnwidth}{@{}lXX@{}}

Method & MAE (months) & $r^2$ \\
\midrule

\textbf{BoneAgeTW2 (ours)}& 10--14     & 0.94 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}
\end{table}

The expected accuracy (10--14 months MAE) is intentionally higher than
Deeplasia's end-to-end MAE because our system is constrained by the TW2
protocol -- routing through discrete bone stages rather than optimizing
directly for bone age. This is acceptable for three reasons:

\begin{enumerate}[noitemsep]
  \item \textbf{Clinical interpretability}: Each bone's stage can be
    reviewed and overridden by the clinician.
  \item \textbf{Protocol compliance}: Many clinical and regulatory contexts
    in Latin America mandate TW2 as the reporting standard.
  \item \textbf{Educational value}: The system trains residents in TW2
    staging by comparing their manual assessment with the AI prediction.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Pseudo-label Quality}

Label quality is highest at ages where stage transitions have low variance.
Accuracy degrades during transition periods ($\pm 1$ year around the mean
transition age). Label smoothing ($\epsilon = 0.1$) mitigates overfitting
to incorrect pseudo-labels.

\section{Ethical Considerations}
\label{sec:ethics}

\begin{itemize}[noitemsep]
  \item The RSNA dataset was collected under IRB-approved protocols.
    Images are de-identified.
  \item BoneAgeTW2 is a \textbf{decision-support tool}, not a replacement
    for clinical judgment. All outputs must be reviewed by a qualified
    radiologist.
  \item The reference population (RSNA, predominantly North American) may
    not represent Latin American populations; a validation study on local
    data is recommended before clinical deployment.
  \item Bone age assessment in forensic contexts should follow professional
    body recommendations regarding method uncertainty~\cite{schmeling2011}.
\end{itemize}

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We presented BoneAgeTW2, the first open-source system to automate the
complete Tanner-Whitehouse 2 protocol for skeletal maturity assessment.
Combining YOLOv8 bone detection, EfficientNet-B3 stage classification
with 20 specialized heads, hardcoded TW2 reference tables, and interactive
Gaussian distribution visualization, the system delivers clinically
interpretable bone age estimates that align with the established
radiological workflow.

The pseudo-label generation strategy enables training on large-scale bone
age datasets (RSNA) despite the absence of per-bone stage annotations,
opening a pathway for future work incorporating datasets with manual TW2
annotations.

\paragraph{Future directions.}
(1) Validation on a Colombian pediatric population. (2) Active learning
to incorporate expert corrections. (3) DICOM SR output for PACS/RIS
integration. (4) Extension to TW3 reference curves.
